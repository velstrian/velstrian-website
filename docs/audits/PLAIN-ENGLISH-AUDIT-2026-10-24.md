# Plain English Review of Velstrian Website

**Date:** 24 October 2026  
**Auditor:** Gemini (Antigravity Agentic Quality Engineering)  
**Perspective:** First-time owner of a 10-person firm who has never bid for a contract  
**Scope:** Complete review of all 48 pages on http://localhost:8093 (source: `crowagent-website/astro/src`)  
**Standard:** Shorter sentences, British English, no jargon without plain explanations, no unproven claims, balanced public and private sector framing, no em dashes, no en dashes, no semicolons.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This audit evaluates the entire Velstrian website through the eyes of a managing director or founder of a 10-person business. This owner runs a growing firm (such as an electrical contractor, IT consultancy, cleaning firm, or design agency) and wants to win new commercial contracts and public sector work. They have never submitted a formal tender before.

When this owner arrives on the website, they encounter four repeated friction points:

1. **Procurement acronyms and legal shorthand:** Terms such as ITT, PPN 026, DPS, framework, PQQ, SQ, and specific section numbers of the Procurement Act 2023 appear without introductory explanation. To a first-time bidder, these sound like an impenetrable regulatory obstacle course.
2. **Public sector bias:** Several pages frame bidding solely around central government, Whitehall guidelines, and town hall tenders. A 10-person firm also bids for private commercial clients, corporate RFPs, and subcontracts for main contractors.
3. **Sentences that run on too long:** Multiple copy blocks contain sentences spanning 30 to 50 words with multiple subordinate clauses, making them difficult to parse on mobile screens between jobs.
4. **Abstract or intimidating evaluation terms:** Phrases like "evaluation criteria" and "deterministic score" distract from the simple question the owner has: "How does the buyer decide who gets the job?"

Every rewrite below follows strict rules: plain British English, simple active phrasing, clear explanations for all technical terms, honest representations of capabilities against `site-facts.json`, and zero semicolons or dashes.

## 4. Key Takeaways for Website Strategy

1. **Speak to the owner's immediate challenge:** A 10-person firm does not care about Whitehall administrative circulars. They care about winning work, not missing deadlines, and not looking foolish in front of a major client.
2. **Normalize commercial work:** Bidding is not exclusive to local councils and government departments. Positioning Velstrian as the go-to tool for private RFPs, commercial subcontracts, and public tenders doubles the product's perceived addressable value.
3. **Keep sentences under 20 words:** Busy business owners skim on phones between client meetings. Short sentences increase comprehension and trust.
4. **Never promise compliance:** Software provides structure, evidence, and speed. It cannot guarantee compliance or winning bids. Maintaining this boundary protects brand integrity and adheres strictly to our verified facts.
