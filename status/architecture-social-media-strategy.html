Social media strategy and automation

Qualify. Draft. Prove it. On every channel, with a human approving every post

A plan for a factual, premium social presence for Velstrian Tenderworks that grows followers and brings small firms into Velstrian Bid and Velstrian Buy. It covers which channels, what to say, how the machinery would work, what it would cost, and what the owner must do. It is a plan only. No account was created, nothing was posted and nothing was bought.

Written 3 November 2026. External facts were read on 3 November 2026 and each carries its link. The company identity used throughout is Velstrian Tenderworks, contact@velstrian.com.

Short answer. Start with LinkedIn (company page plus founder profile), then YouTube and X, and add the rest later. Run it as a draft, check, approve, post line. Our own AI client drafts, a second model plus a source fetch checks every claim, and every post is emailed through Brevo to aparajita.parihar@gmail.com with approve and reject links. Nothing posts without that click. The recommended stack costs about 0 pounds a month in tool fees and an estimated under 5 US dollars a month in AI and X API usage (an estimate from assumed token counts, shown in section 3). Building the scheduler needs the owner to approve the RULE 0-G cost statement in section 3 first. Until then, phase 0 is manual and free.

Tone. Plain English first, as RULE 0-U requires. One idea per post, one action. No jargon unless explained once. Name public and private work together. Positive wording, no defensive disclaimers (D255). Formats. Carousels for craft, short voiced video for product, polls for engagement, threads only for sourced news. Hashtags. Two or three per post, from a short fixed list such as #bidwriting #tenders #UKprocurement, reviewed monthly. More than three reads as spam. Hashtag reach effects were not measured.

Rules for 100% factual accuracy

1
Every claim carries a source URL. The generator must output a claims list, each claim with a URL. A post with an unsourced factual claim is rejected by code before it reaches the approver.
2
No news without its source. News posts are only created from a fetched page. No source fetched, no post. Nothing is written from the model's memory.
3
No invented customer results. Customer proof needs a consent record. We have no customers' results yet, so nothing claims any.
4
Dates and numbers are copied from the source. A check compares every number and date in the post to the fetched text. Any mismatch blocks the post.
5
A person approves every post. No auto posting, no exceptions, including replies and reposts.

Section 3Automation architecture, stack and the RULE 0-G cost statement

The line, end to end

1
Plan. A weekly job reads the calendar and the source list (gov.uk pages, our site) and picks the topics.
2
Draft. Our own AIClient (class at api/app/services/ai/client.py line 516) drafts each post with its claims list. It already carries a spend ceiling and reservation mixins, so a monthly cap for this job fits the existing pattern. Not re-measured beyond the class definition.
3
Fact check. Code fetches every source URL and confirms the page loads. A second, cheaper model checks each claim against the fetched text and must answer supported, not supported, or cannot tell. Anything but supported blocks the post. This is a control, not a guarantee, so the human approval stays.
4
Approval email through Brevo. Each post goes as one email to aparajita.parihar@gmail.com with the text, the sources, the check result and two links, Approve and Reject. The links open a confirmation page and the click that posts is a button press on that page, not the link itself, because email scanners open links automatically. Links are signed, single use and expire after a set time. Reject needs no reason.
5
Schedule and post. Only an approved post is sent to the posting layer. The post time is fixed by the calendar. The system never posts a draft, a rejected post or an expired one.
6
Measure. Every link back to velstrian.com carries UTM tags. PostHog records visits and signups. Brevo records newsletter growth.

The token counts below are our assumptions, not measurements. Draft: 3,000 in and 1,000 out on Sonnet = $0.006 + $0.010 = $0.016. Routines

  • Commenting. 10 thoughtful comments a day by a person on posts from buyers, procurement bodies and small firms. Comments are never automated.
  • Community. Answer real bidding questions on Reddit and LinkedIn groups with no link unless asked, after reading each community's rules.
  • Collaboration. Invite procurement trainers and supplier support groups to short joint videos or guest posts. Ask first, never tag people without consent.
  • Employee and founder advocacy. The founder and any team post their own view. The company page shares it. LinkedIn's employee advocacy features exist in the Community Management API (same Microsoft Learn page), but a person-level posting permission is closed, so employees post by hand.

Section 5Risks and controls

RiskControl
AI generated misinformationClaims list with a URL per claim, fetch of every source, second model check, number and date match, and a human approval. A rejected post is logged with its reason, and a rate of rejections above a set level pauses the line.
Brand safetyA banned topics list (politics, named competitors, named individuals), no replies or reposts generated by AI, tone guide in the prompt, and the approver reads the whole post. Keep a record of every approved version.
Platform bans for automationPosting only through official APIs or an approved scheduler, no scraping, no automated likes, follows or comments, and limits kept well under the figures stated on the platform pages above. Platform terms for X, Meta and YouTube must be read in full at build time. Only extracts were read for this page.
GDPR in analyticsUTM tags carry no personal data. PostHog configured for consent and for the UK or EU region. Brevo newsletter signups use an explicit opt in and the privacy page. Poll and comment data is not copied into our systems. A data protection check by the owner before launch. This is a plan, not legal advice.
Impersonation and fraud accountsClaim our names early on every platform in section 1. Publish the official list of our handles on velstrian.com. Contact only comes from contact@velstrian.com and we never ask for payment or passwords in a message. Report fake accounts through each platform. Watch for lookalike names weekly by hand. Approval link abuseSigned, single use, expiring tokens, confirmation page with a button, approver address fixed in config, and every approval logged with time.
