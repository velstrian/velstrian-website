Qualify. Draft. Prove it. On every channel, with a human approving every post

Rules for 100% factual accuracy

1
Every claim carries a source URL. The generator must output a claims list, each claim with a URL. A post with an unsourced factual claim is rejected by code before it reaches the approver.
2
No news without its source. News posts are only created from a fetched page. No source fetched, no post. Nothing is written from the model's memory.
3
No invented customer results. Customer proof needs a consent record. We have no customers' results yet, so nothing claims any.
4
Dates and numbers are copied from the source. A check compares every number and date in the post to the fetched text. Any mismatch blocks the post.
5
A person approves every post. No auto posting, no exceptions, including replies and reposts.

Section 3Automation architecture, stack and the RULE 0-G cost statement

The line, end to end

1
Plan. A weekly job reads the calendar and the source list (gov.uk pages, our site) and picks the topics.
2
Draft. Our own AIClient (class at api/app/services/ai/client.py line 516) drafts each post with its claims list. It already carries a spend ceiling and reservation mixins, so a monthly cap for this job fits the existing pattern. Not re-measured beyond the class definition.
3
Fact check. Code fetches every source URL and confirms the page loads. A second, cheaper model checks each claim against the fetched text and must answer supported, not supported, or cannot tell. Anything but supported blocks the post. This is a control, not a guarantee, so the human approval stays.
4
Approval email through Brevo. Each post goes as one email to aparajita.parihar@gmail.com with the text, the sources, the check result and two links, Approve and Reject. The links open a confirmation page and the click that posts is a button press on that page, not the link itself, because email scanners open links automatically. Links are signed, single use and expire after a set time. Reject needs no reason.
5
Schedule and post. Only an approved post is sent to the posting layer. The post time is fixed by the calendar. The system never posts a draft, a rejected post or an expired one.
6
Measure. Every link back to velstrian.com carries UTM tags. PostHog records visits and signups. Brevo records newsletter growth.

Which AI is needed and what it costs

The job needs two small text calls per post: a draft of about 1,000 words in and out, and a claim check. Both are cheap models. The Gemini pricing page lists a free tier for Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (pages read 3 November 2026 at aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing), and our AIClient already imports Gemini 3.8 Flash (api/app/services/ai/client.py line 118). So drafting and checking use the existing Gemini route on a free tier key, at GBP 0. About 65 posts a month is a very small volume. The per model free request limits were not on the page excerpt, so they are read in Google AI Studio at build.

Privacy, stated plainly. The pricing page marks free tier content as used to improve Google products, and the terms page says human reviewers may read and annotate unpaid service input and output after it is detached from the account, and tells users not to send sensitive, confidential or personal information. The same terms page says the Paid Services data terms apply to Unpaid Services in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK. Either way our prompts hold only public source text (gov.uk pages and our own site) and public draft posts. No customer data, no private documents and no personal data is ever sent. A rule in the generator enforces that the fact list can only come from public URLs.

The Anthropic key stays the fallback and is not used in Phase 1. At the earlier assumptions (our estimate, not a measurement) it would cost about 1.3 US dollars a month.
