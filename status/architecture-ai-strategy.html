Section 1The verdict, and what matters most

The honest verdict

The AI is careful and honest. It refuses any number the supplier never gave, it drops a tender requirement it cannot quote word for word, and it returns nothing rather than guessing when your documents hold no evidence. It is held back by four things: almost nothing is measured, one provider carries nearly everything, an invented certificate or client is only noted, and nobody yet owns AI quality as a running service. A menu of models would fix none of the four. Quality comes from our own search, knowledge and checks, which work the same whatever model writes the text. That is where the effort goes.

1. Biggest risk

One provider, legacy models

Gemini 2.5 runs nearly every customer feature. Google limits 2.5 to past users and gives no notice period in days. Claude is already in the DPA and could stand behind it, once D339 lifts.

2. Decided, to build

Credential cards, never a block

D341: a credential your record does not show gets a card beside the sentence: we hold it, underway, remove, or keep with a comment. Recorded for the PPN 017 statement.

3. Decided, to build

Every draft checked, "backed by N"

D342: claim backing runs after every draft and shows how many of your documents back it. The cost per draft is measured and shown before it is switched on.

4. Missing

Nobody owns AI quality yet

No weekly review, no incident runbook, no support playbook for "the AI got it wrong". Section 19 proposes a model for a tiny team. Decision AI QUALITY OWNER.

5. Missing

No targets, no dashboard

Search recall has never scored. No quality target is agreed per feature. Section 13 proposes targets and a free PostHog dashboard with counts only.

6. Keep it simple

Auto first, three plain levels

Every comparable product defaults to Auto. Fast, Standard and Best on the first screen. Vendor names, OpenAI included, behind an advanced panel.

Why this is the moat, and what a competitor cannot copy quickly

Any competitor can rent the same models at the same prices. What they cannot rent is a dated procurement knowledge base checked against its sources, a test set built from real public tenders, checks that refuse invented figures and credentials, and counts from many bids showing where drafts go wrong. Those compound with use. The model is the replaceable part, which is also why a second provider is cheap to add once the evidence layer is neutral.

Honest limit: layers 2 and 3 are only as good as their measurement. Until recall and the deep tests have numbers (sections 13 and 15), "smart" is a design, not a measured result, and public copy should say what the AI does, not how accurate it is (website rule: no AI accuracy figures).

What OpenAI adds that Anthropic and Google do not, honestly

A third, independent checker

A model tends to rate its own family's writing higher. With Google writing and Claude as the second provider, OpenAI is a judge from a family that wrote neither draft. Useful for the quality gate and the second opinion.

The only EU processing route

OpenAI is the one provider that documents EU processing (eu.api.openai.com), by approval. D336 says do not apply now. It is the option if a buyer insists.

The longest notice

Six months before a general model retires, against 60 days for Anthropic and no number for Google. The least churn of the three.

No price or proven quality edge

Its price points match Anthropic's at every level. Nobody has measured it on our tasks. A buyer policy that names OpenAI is plausible, as Microsoft 365 Copilot defaults to it, but no such buyer has been found.

Not needed for resilience

The biggest risk, one provider, is fixed by putting Claude behind Gemini. Claude is already in the DPA. OpenAI is a third adapter, a third deprecation feed, a DPA entry, a notice and a legal review.

The honest view

Worth adding, second. Anthropic fallback first, then OpenAI as the independent judge and a choice for Pro and Portfolio, only if its family eval passes. Decision OPENAI SEQUENCE.

Section 6What customers gain from more than one model

The research looked for evidence that letting business users choose a model improves what they get. It found none. Vendors argue it does and publish no data. What is known points the other way for non experts: users overestimate how accurate AI answers are, and longer answers raise their confidence without raising accuracy. A small business owner who picks a "better" model cannot tell from the draft whether it was better.

Real gain for the customerWhy it holds
The AI keeps working when one provider failsToday a Gemini outage stops almost all customer AI. A second provider behind the same checks is the largest single benefit, and the customer never has to choose anything to get it
A second opinion from a different vendorA model tends to rate its own family's writing higher (self preference bias, measured in published studies). A checker from another vendor catches different mistakes. Three vendors make an independent judge always available
Speed or depth when it mattersA quick summary does not need the most expensive model. A final executive summary might. Three plain levels let a user say so
A buyer or policy that names a vendorSome organisations allow only certain AI providers. An admin control that limits providers serves them
Fewer surprises when vendors changeModels are retired on vendor timetables. A registry and a test gate mean a retirement becomes a planned swap, not an outage
Not a real gainWhy
"Pick the best model and get fewer mistakes"A better model lowers the error rate, it does not remove it. Legal research tools sold as hallucination free were measured wrong 17 to 33 percent of the time. Our checks after the model matter more than the model
A long list of vendors and versionsIt moves the risk and the cost onto the user, who cannot judge the result. No bid writing tool in the research showed a model picker at all

Section 9The model and provider lifecycle

Vendors retire and reprice models on their own timetable, so changing a model has to be a routine chore with a gate, not an emergency. One path, the same for every model and all three providers. Nothing here is built. All of it is on hold under D339. The rules it follows are recorded: D322 (only passed models are shown), D328 (models come from the registry), D332 (drafting family eval budget about GBP 10), D333 (lists pulled on demand, never on a timer) and D335 (legacy migration).

1
Discover, on demand. A script and an admin button read the three providers' model lists. No timer, because a timer spends money while nobody watches (D333, RULE 0-G). A new model found this way is shown to nobody. OpenAI's list gives only a shutdown date, so its prices and limits are entered by a person.
2
Register. Saved as a candidate with its limits, its retirement date and a dated price row. Prices change on known dates, so a price without a date is a defect.
3
Quality gate. Deterministic checks first, because they are free and cannot be flattered. Then the golden set for that family of features (drafting, extraction, ranking, chat). Scored by a judge from a different vendor than the writer, with answer order swapped. The pass rule is relative: match or beat the current approved model on our own cases, with zero regressions on the figure and claim checks. Run by hand with a stated cap. Never on OpenAI's Evals platform, which shuts on 30 November 2026.
4
Approve. A person signs it off and the sign off is recorded. Only then is the model visible, only for the families it passed (D322), and only if its provider is in DPA Schedule 1 (D328).
5
Serve and monitor. Each call names the model, the prompt version and why it was chosen. Content free signals per feature. A fallback chain across two providers, and the user told when the fallback served them.
6
Deprecate. On a vendor notice, a price step that breaks the credit weight, or failing checks. Users who had chosen it move to Auto with a one line notice.
7
Retire. Hidden and never called again. The record stays so an old bid can still say which model wrote it.
What the lifecycle deliberately leaves out

No live canary or shadow traffic. Sending 5 percent of real traffic to an untested model puts customers at risk and needs volume Velstrian does not have. The cheaper equivalent is an offline replay of the golden set before approval. D349 puts canary in the backlog.

No aliases. Always call an explicit id from the registry, never "latest". Whether an undated Anthropic 5.x id can change underneath us is not stated by Anthropic, so the id is the unit that passes the gate.

The eval budget goes to the harness first. D308 shows the bigger cost is test reliability (986 of 1,709 rows failed before reaching the model), not model calls. D343 fixes the harness offline first so live spend buys real signal.
