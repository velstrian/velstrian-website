AI strategy

How Velstrian uses AI, how we keep it honest, and what to do next

Velstrian Bid and Velstrian Buy use AI to read tenders, draft answers from your own documents and check what was written. This page says what the AI does today, how good it is, what customers would really gain from a choice of models, how a model is tested before anyone sees it, and how the AI gets better over time without using customer data. Plain words first. The expert detail sits behind the plus signs.

Section 1The verdict, and the five things that matter most

The honest verdict

The AI is careful and honest. It refuses any number the supplier never gave, it drops a tender requirement it cannot quote word for word, and it returns nothing rather than guessing when your documents hold no evidence. It is held back by three things: almost nothing is measured, one provider carries nearly everything, and an invented certificate or client is only noted, not stopped. A menu of models would fix none of those three. Quality comes from our own search and checks, which work the same whatever model writes the text.

1. Biggest risk

One provider, legacy models

Gemini 2.5 runs nearly every customer feature. Google limits 2.5 to past users and gives no notice period in days. An outage or access loss stops almost all customer AI at once.

2. Fix now

Invented credentials are only noted

A draft saying "We hold ISO 27001" when you do not can be copied into a bid with a note beside it. The check is a fixed list of 18 names on 3 drafting features. This is not new model work.

3. Fix now

The claim check is a button

The only check that reads every claim against your evidence runs when you press it. Make it run after every draft and show "backed by 9 of your documents".

4. Measure

Search quality is unknown

The search is well built and has never been scored. The cut off was fitted on 10 rows from one organisation. The 90 percent target has never produced a number.

5. Keep it simple

Auto first, three plain levels

Every comparable product defaults to Auto. Starter gets Auto only. Pro and Portfolio may pick Fast, Standard or Best. Frontier models only on Portfolio. Vendor names behind an advanced panel.

Section 2What customers gain from more than one model, and what they do not

The research looked for evidence that letting business users choose a model improves what they get. It found none. Vendors argue it does and publish no data. What is known points the other way for non experts: users overestimate how accurate AI answers are, and longer answers raise their confidence without raising accuracy. A small business owner who picks a "better" model cannot tell from the draft whether it was better.

The test evidence behind the scores (D308 and earlier runs)

The D308 golden run on staging wrote 1,709 rows. 986 were errors from the test harness (login and organisation lookups, daily ceilings, a retired route), not from the model. Only 9 errors touched the model path. Taking the latest row per repetition: 632 repetitions, 368 PASS, 180 RECORDED, 32 FAIL, 52 ERROR. Twelve fixes landed on 24 October and none has a live recheck yet.

A correction the audit makes to the tracker: the tracker says every FAIL was a product finding. Two clusters were partly test defects, a missing ground truth label (6a5f408f3) and a harness that withheld 5 of 13 profile fields (8bc5924bc). The product fixes are still real, but the FAIL count overstates product failure on those two.

Earlier: 13 September, 309 rows, 274 PASS and 35 FAIL. 22 October (D277), 51 rows, 50 PASS and 1 ERROR. Every D308 row reads "dirty tree", so a result cannot be tied to one exact commit.

Not covered at all: real customer tenders in the live set (8 real Contracts Finder documents exist but do not gate), private sector (2 of 11 scenarios), Welsh, long or scanned packs, and any human rating of usefulness.

Section 5The permanent model and provider lifecycle

Vendors retire and reprice models on their own timetable, so changing a model has to be a routine chore with a gate, not an emergency. One path, the same for every model and every provider. Nothing here is built. All of it is on hold under D339.

1
Discover, on demand. A script and an admin button read the three providers' model lists. No timer, because a timer spends money while nobody watches (D333, RULE 0-G). A new model found this way is shown to nobody.
2
Register. Saved as a candidate with its limits, its retirement date and a dated price row. Prices change on known dates, so a price without a date is a defect.
3
Quality gate. Deterministic checks first, because they are free and cannot be flattered: does the adapter answer, does the figure check pass, does the claim check find zero invented claims. Then the golden set for that family of features (drafting, extraction, ranking, chat). Scored by a judge from a different vendor than the writer, with answer order swapped. The pass rule is relative: match or beat the current approved model on our own cases, with zero regressions on the figure and claim checks. Cheap family first, and a model that fails it skips the rest. Run by hand with a stated cap (D332 approved about GBP 10 for the drafting family, the full matrix is about GBP 39).
4
Approve. A person signs it off and the sign off is recorded. Only then is the model visible, and only for the families it passed (D322). It must also have its provider in the data processing agreement (D328).
5
Serve and monitor. Each call names the model, the prompt version and why it was chosen. Content free signals per feature (how often the figure check fires, how many claims lack backing). A fallback chain across two providers, and the user told when the fallback served them.
6
Deprecate. On a vendor notice, a price step that breaks the credit weight, or failing checks. Users who had chosen it move to Auto with a one line notice.
7
Retire. Hidden and never called again. The record stays so an old bid can still say which model wrote it.

What the lifecycle deliberately leaves out

No live canary or shadow traffic. Sending 5 percent of real traffic to an untested model puts customers at risk and needs volume Velstrian does not have. The cheaper equivalent is an offline replay of the golden set before approval. Shadow testing doubles spend on every shadowed call and waits until usage justifies it.

No aliases. Always call an explicit id from the registry, never "latest". Whether an undated Anthropic 5.x id can change underneath us is not stated by Anthropic, so the id is the unit that passes the gate.

The eval budget goes to the harness first. D308 shows the bigger cost is test reliability (986 of 1,709 rows failed before reaching the model), not model calls. The design paper sized the eval burden from model cost alone, and the audit disagrees.

Section 7Getting better over time without customer data

Velstrian uses hosted models, so there is nothing of our own to train. "Getting better" means better search, better prompts, better checks and a bigger test set, all of which help every model and every customer at once. When a customer uploads bid documents, Velstrian is the processor. Using that content to improve the service for others would be a new purpose of our own, and the ICO says a processor that sets its own purposes becomes a controller. So the rule is simple: customer content is never used for improvement by default.

Section 10What may have been missed

A bidder can attack Buy through its own reply

Buy reads replies that suppliers wrote. A hidden instruction in a PDF could try to inflate that supplier's comparison. Test it before Buy is sold widely.

Churn is routine, not rare

Three price steps and two retirements fall inside the next 10 weeks. Plan a migration chore every quarter and store dated prices.

Cost that never reaches the ledger

When a ledger write fails the spend is logged and lost. D308 recorded GBP 2.83 against a true GBP 4.18. The ceiling still holds, every cost report reads low.

Low volume features are never judged

The weekly health check needs 20 calls a week for a verdict. Only 1 of 28 features reaches that, so a quiet feature can be broken for life unseen.

An opening competitors did not show

Buyers are being told to ask about AI use. A one page statement from what we already log answers that question honestly in one click.

Data residency honesty

No provider offers UK processing on the APIs used. Google may cache paid data in any country it operates. Claim only what we have, some buyers will ask.

Test results tied to no commit

Every D308 row says "dirty tree". Run golden tests from a committed tree so a result maps to a sha.

Private sector and Welsh are barely tested

The product sells to private buyers too, and 2 of 11 test scenarios mention them. No Welsh case exists.

Where the two source files disagree, and what this page follows

Gemini paid tier. The research says to confirm the Gemini key is on the paid service, because Google's no training promise depends on it. The audit says the owner verified both production accounts as paid and billing enabled (D314). Production is therefore confirmed. The data map row R285-TRUST-AI-PROVIDER-DATA-MAP-01 still asks for both keys, so the staging key is the open check.

Eval shape. The research says the existing setup "already matches" the mature eval pattern. The audit agrees on shape and finds it weak in practice: the gate only observes, samples are small and harness faults outnumbered model results. This page follows the audit on the state.

Lifecycle priority. The design paper ranks Haiku 4.5 as the urgent legacy model. The audit ranks Gemini 2.5 higher, because it carries almost every customer feature while Haiku 4.5 serves only internal and dead surfaces. D335 still says Haiku first. Both orders are offered in decision 8.

DPA Anthropic list. The feasibility study relied on the DPA naming Anthropic for bid_eval_judge and copilot_intent. Neither has a production caller, so the DPA text should rest on buy_preread alone.
