Google Vertex credits plan, research 2026-10-26

Row: R285-P3-AI-VERTEX-CREDITS-PLAN-01. Owner decision D465. Research only. Nothing was spent, built, enabled or changed. Writing style RULE 0-S.

Owner question: "check how you can leverage google vertex credits to make crowagent as top 1% ultra premium best SaaS enterprise". RULE 0-U sets the test: every option below is judged by whether it makes bid work simpler or more trustworthy for a small business that bids for itself, not by how impressive the Google product list is.

Answer in six lines

  1. The only credit we hold today is the Google Cloud Free Trial: USD 300, 90 days, ending about 24 Jan 2027 (the date in docs/release-2.8.5/ai-strategy/VERTEX-VS-GEMINI-API-2026-10-25.md line 16). USD 300 is about GBP 237 at the platform rate of 0.79 (api/app/services/ai/pricing.py line 88). The Part D check of whether it actually paid for a Vertex call has no recorded result yet, so that is still UNVERIFIED.
  2. The big prize is the Google for Startups Cloud Program AI tier: up to USD 350,000 over two years. It is a separate application, it needs venture funding and a company age we have not confirmed, and Google decides.
  3. Credits will not pay for Claude. Google's own pages say third party models are billed directly and are not covered by programme credits, and the Free Trial excludes partner models offered as a managed API. So "Claude on Vertex paid from credits" is not available. This is the most important correction to the idea.
  4. Credits can plausibly pay for Gemini on Vertex. That is a real saving, but at our volume the saving is small (the whole D411 Claude test session and the D450 Gemini test session together cost about GBP 9). The credits are worth far less than the residency answer.
  5. The strongest product reason to move Gemini to Vertex is data location, not money. Gemini 3.8 Flash, 3.1 Pro, 3.5 Flash-Lite and both embedding models are listed for europe-west2 (London) and the eu multi-region. Our DPA says Gemini is not region pinned (docs/legal/data-processing-agreement.md line 378).
  6. The tender reading gap (D388 recall 80.7 percent, critical recall 78.4 percent) is not proven to be a PDF parsing problem. D450 moved ryde_gdpr recall from 24.7 to 91.8 percent by prompt alone. Document AI is therefore a measured test first, not a purchase.

Why Document AI is a test, not a purchase

D450 shows the extraction gap moves with the prompt. Run 4 to run 5: ryde_gdpr recall went from 37.0 to 91.8 percent and critical recall to 100 percent by adding one sentence to the v7 prompt. The remaining D388 failures are walkwheel_survey (critical recall 57.9 to 63.2 percent) and rdash_town_planning (critical recall 66.0 percent, 27 critical errors), and run to run variance on 3.1 Pro is large (walkwheel 87 rows in one run, 49 in the next). Better input structure could fix part of that, but nobody has measured whether the missed rows sat in tables or in plain text. A free first step is to classify the missed critical rows of rdash and walkwheel by where they sit in the source PDF. Only if most sit in tables or headers does the paid A/B make sense.

4. Ranked recommendation (questions for the owner)

1. Apply to the Google for Startups Cloud Program, AI tier, and finish the Free Trial Part D check. Gives: a funded runway of up to USD 350,000 if accepted, and the fact on whether trial credit pays for Vertex. Effort: one application (owner account action) and reading one billing report. Credits burn: none. Question: Is Velstrian Tenderworks eligible (funding stage, company age, prior Google Cloud credits) and do you want to apply? Recommended: yes, answer every question truthfully, and do not describe the company as venture funded if it is not.

2. Run Gemini and embeddings on Vertex in the eu multi-region behind an environment flag, staging first. Gives: a stated processing location for the DPA and sales answers, plus the credit. Effort: about 6 files plus service account handling, medium risk until batch and embeddings are tried live (earlier doc, item 3). Credits burn: small, test traffic plus staging runs. Question: After Part D shows whether credit paid, do you approve a staging only build of the Vertex client with the existing key path kept as the default? Recommended: yes, and keep production on the Gemini API until the staging walk and a golden subset pass.

3. Classify the missed D388 rows by location in the PDF, then run a Document AI Layout Parser A/B on the failing tenders only. Gives: evidence on whether structure fixes critical recall, the one number the tender reading bar still lacks. Effort: classification is a read only job, the A/B needs a pre pass and a reader change. Credits burn: classification nil, the A/B is the page count times USD 0.01. For two tenders of about 100 pages it is about USD 2 (assumed page counts). Question: Do you approve a one off Document AI test capped at GBP 12 once the classification says tables are the cause? Recommended: yes, conditional on the classification.

4. Switch on Vertex context caching for the large surfaces and set Vertex batch for the nightly jobs. Gives: lower repeat cost on tender chunk prompts and evidence heavy drafts. Effort: small once option 2 exists. Credits burn: reduces spend. Question: Do you want caching measured on bid_rfp_extract and bid_response_draft before it is made default? Recommended: yes, measured on the golden harness first (RULE 0-T applies).

5. Pilot Google Search grounding on regulatory_monitor only. Gives: fresher legal and policy change watching for buyers and bidders, which is a clear customer story. Effort: one surface and a source display rule. Credits burn: low. Question: Do you accept live web sources on a single background surface if every claim shows its source and never replaces the curated PPN 026 knowledge base? Recommended: yes for the pilot, no for customer facing answers.
