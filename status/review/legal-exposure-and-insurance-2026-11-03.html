Legal exposure, insurance at minimal cost, and fraud safeguards

Row R285-OWNER-INSURER-01. Prepared 2026-11-03 for the owner of Velstrian Tenderworks (Velstrian Systems Ltd). General guidance, not regulated advice. A broker or solicitor confirms the final choice (said once, here).

Short answer

  • We publish 11 legal pages, about 16,000 words. The law requires far fewer. Most of the rest is market practice, and each extra promise is something a buyer can hold us to.
  • Law requires: a privacy notice, a cookie notice (only because we use non-essential cookies), company details on the site, and consumer terms only if consumers buy. Everything else is optional.
  • Eight comparator sites checked (not 50): 2 to 5 footer legal pages, caps almost always 12 months fees, four of five terms read have no SLA.
  • Biggest live exposures today: a "99.5% uptime" line and an "8 hours outage credit" in the refund policy (an SLA by the back door), a "within 72 hours" breach promise and "aim to restore within 24 hours" in the DPA, and a leftover "GDPR compliant" line in security.md.
  • Recommended (owner direction D531): publish Privacy, Terms, Cookies and a short Security page. Keep DPA, sub-processors, retention and residency written and ready, available on request via one footer line. Soften every number to a target.
  • Insurance: nothing is compulsory today. Employers' liability becomes compulsory with the first employee, possibly not for a sole director who owns the company (unverified, ask the broker).
  • Cheapest sensible bundle now: tech PI plus cyber, about GBP 300 to 1,000 a year for GBP 250k to 1m cover, based on published "from" prices and benchmarks (sources below). Treat as indicative only.
  • Our Terms cap data protection claims at GBP 250,000, but a cheap online policy may only give GBP 25,000 cyber. Fix: keep the cap but make it "the lesser of twice fees and GBP 250,000", and buy cover to match once revenue exists.
  • The broker email drafts need three edits before sending (turnover, and the liability wording, which no longer matches the live Terms).
  • Fraud: the product has a bot check on sign-in, rate limiting and a trial rule, but I found no card-testing, dispute handling or Radar evidence in code. Those are the gaps.

A2. Required versus market practice

Required by law (sources: ICO right to be informed, ICO cookies guidance, E-Commerce Regulations 2002 reg 6):

  • Privacy notice (UK GDPR transparency).
  • Cookie information and consent for non-essential cookies (PECR).
  • Name, address, company number and contact email of the company on the site.
  • Consumer Contracts Regulations information only if consumers buy. We sell to businesses, so a B2B-only statement in Terms is enough.

Market practice only: DPA, sub-processor list, acceptable use policy, SLA, security page, AI policy, retention schedule, residency note, accessibility statement (the last is expected by public buyers, not required of a private company).

Comparators, read from their footers on 2026-11-03: autogenai.com (privacy, usage, cookie, app cookie, customer T&C, app T&C, app privacy, accessibility, trust centre in nav), mytender.io (terms, data protection, privacy, trust centre), tenderbase.co.uk (terms, privacy only). None list a DPA or sub-processor page in the footer.

A3. Risks either way

Over-publishing: a published number becomes a promise. Examples live now, all in C:/w/vw/astro/src/content/legal: "99.5% monthly uptime" (security.md line 119, worded as a target, good), outage credit after 8 hours (refund-policy.md line 25, reads as a commitment), breach notice "within 72 hours" (dpa.md line 52), restore "within 24" hours (dpa.md line 70), "GDPR compliant" (security.md line 141, removed from the footer on 2026-08-05 for being unbacked, still in the page), 14 day full refund (refund-policy.md line 15) that contradicts the "no refunds" line in Terms (terms.md line 57). Contradictions are the worst kind of exposure.

Under-publishing: no privacy notice or cookie notice invites ICO action, and missing trust pages cost deals with buyers who send security questionnaires.

Part B. Insurance at minimal cost

B1. Which covers matter

  • Compulsory now: none. Employers' liability is compulsory from the day you employ anyone, minimum GBP 5m, fine GBP 2,500 per day without it (GOV.UK). GOV.UK lists only family members and staff based outside Great Britain as exempt. Several broker sites say a company whose only employee is a director owning at least half the shares is exempt, but I could not confirm that on an official page, so ask the broker before relying on it.
  • Matters for us: professional indemnity including tech errors and omissions (the AI output risk), cyber and data breach, public and products liability (low risk for software but often demanded by buyers), directors and officers (lowest priority until there is outside funding).

B3. Exclusions to ask about

AI-generated content errors (ask for explicit confirmation that AI-drafted output is covered, not excluded), liability taken on by contract beyond what the law would impose, regulatory fines (often uninsurable), claims known before the start date, and customers in the US (many UK policies exclude or limit US jurisdiction).

B4. Matching the liability cap to cover

Cover cannot be bought for a promise you have not made, and a promise you cannot insure is paid from your own pocket. Our live Terms (terms.md lines 106 to 114) cap general claims at 12 months fees with no floor and data protection claims at twice that, never above GBP 250,000. At year 1 revenue under GBP 50k, twice fees is at most about GBP 100k, so the real number is far below GBP 250k. Options:

  1. Keep the cap and buy GBP 250k cyber. Safe but dearer.
  2. Change to "the lesser of twice fees and GBP 250,000". This is what the cap already effectively does while revenue is low. Recommended, and no extra cover is needed beyond the first policy.
  3. Link the cap to the insurance recovered. The broker drafts describe this, but it is not in the live Terms. It makes customers uneasy and invites disputes. Not recommended.

B5. Bundle and timing

  • Now (no customers): cheapest sensible is nothing bound yet, quotes only. Buy at the first paying customer: tech PI plus cyber at GBP 250k to 1m, one combined policy, about GBP 300 to 1,000 a year on the evidence above.
  • Add public and products liability at the first buyer who asks.
  • Add employers' liability on the first employee.
  • First public sector contract: expect GBP 1m each for PI and public liability and GBP 5m employers' liability. G-Cloud 14 call-off terms set PI at GBP 1,000,000 per claim with six years of tail cover, after Crown Commercial Service dropped an earlier GBP 10m idea (Computer Weekly). Lot 4 and G-Cloud 15 figures were not confirmed, check the live framework. Individual tenders may ask GBP 5m.
  • Cyber Essentials may unlock free cover: one broker source says GBP 25,000 free cyber for firms under GBP 20m turnover with the certificate (Assured Cyber Protection). Unverified on the official scheme page, check before planning around it.

B6. Steps

Two broker routes through BIBA find a broker (pick one tech specialist and one generalist) and two direct online options, Simply Business and Hiscox. Have ready: turnover forecast, activities, data volumes and types, controls (MFA, backups, encryption, EU hosting), contract terms, no prior claims.

Broker drafts (owner-drafts/BROKER-EMAIL-DRAFT-1-2026-11-01.md and -2): they are identical apart from placeholders and read well. Changes needed:

  1. Fill the turnover band (still a placeholder).
  2. The "Our contract terms" paragraph states a GBP 2,500 floor and "insurance proceeds actually recovered". The live Terms have neither. Make the email match the live Terms, or change the Terms first.
  3. Add a question on whether AI-generated output errors are covered or excluded, and on US claims (the draft only asks about non-UK customers generally).
