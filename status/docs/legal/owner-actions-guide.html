4. International privacy addenda (package 8): do you need a law firm?

Row: R285-INTL-PRIVATE-RFP-GLOBAL-01. Full draft: docs/release-2.8.5/legal/PACKAGE-8-INTL-DPA-ADDENDA-DRAFT-2026-10-23.md.

Why a law firm was suggested, in plain words

Claude can draft the paper and has. Claude cannot give legal advice and cannot take legal responsibility if a draft is wrong. Privacy laws in other countries carry real fines, and a few points in the draft are marked "not verified here" because the primary text was not read. A lawyer's job is to confirm those points and stand behind the answer. That is the only reason. It is not required before you have a customer there.

The no law firm path (recommended)

This path is genuinely usable. It costs nothing until it is needed.

Step A. Do nothing now. Until a customer outside the UK is about to pay, no addendum is needed. Your existing DPA already covers UK and EU GDPR, which includes Ireland. Meanwhile your sales pages should say plainly: "UK and EU GDPR data processing agreement available now, local law addenda on request." Claude can add that line when you say yes (it is decision D-E in the draft).

Step B. When the first customer outside the UK is close to signing, use the official templates and the draft. Be clear about what each template is for, because they are often confused:

  • The UK IDTA and the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs are published by the ICO. They are the tools for a UK business that sends personal data out of the UK to a country without UK adequacy. The ICO says you can choose either, you cannot drop the mandatory clauses, and you must also complete a transfer risk assessment (source: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/appropriate-safeguards/what-are-standard-data-protection-clauses-the-uk-idta-and-the-addendum/). For you this covers Velstrian's own sending to US providers such as Anthropic and Google, and your DPA clause 7 already points at it. It is not the document a customer in Australia signs to send data to you.
  • The EU standard contractual clauses (Commission Implementing Decision 2021/914) are for a data exporter in the EU sending to an importer outside the EU that the GDPR does not already cover (source: https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32021D0914, a secondary copy of the decision, so confirm on EUR-Lex). Velstrian sits in the UK, and a secondary list of the Commission's adequacy decisions includes the UK (source: https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/the-list-of-countries-deemed-adequate-for-gdpr-transfers-table/59031, undated, so confirm on the Commission page https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_lv). If that holds, an Irish or other EU customer needs your DPA and no SCCs. Do not rely on this until you or a lawyer has checked the Commission page.
