B. The liability caps in the Terms. File: crowagent-website/astro/src/content/legal/terms.md, section 6, changed in website commit {{SHA_WEBSITE_1}} (local only, not deployed). Owner decision D314, option B, insurance backed:

  • General commercial breach, bugs and service interruptions: "the greater of (a) the total subscription fees paid by you in the 12 months preceding the claim, or (b) £2,500" (was £1,000).
  • Data protection and security breaches under the DPA, a separate and distinct cap: "the lesser of (a) £250,000, or (b) the actual proceeds recovered under Velstrian's Cyber / Technology Professional Indemnity insurance policy for that claim".

  • No indemnity to the customer for regulatory fines or penalties imposed by any supervisory authority, for example the ICO.
  • Not limited: death or personal injury caused by negligence, fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, anything that cannot be limited by law.

The DPA clause 11 carries the same two caps. The website is not deployed from this pack (RULE 0-G and the website rule: nothing goes to the production website until the owner approves).

4. Points for counsel

  1. The data protection cap can fall to £0. Drafting risk identified by the driver. The cap is the LESSER of £250,000 or the insurance proceeds actually recovered. If the insurer pays nothing (a declined claim, a policy exclusion, or no policy in force), the effective cap is £0. A customer's counsel may resist that, and it may not pass the reasonableness test in the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977. The common alternative is "the greater of a fixed floor and the insurance proceeds". Counsel to advise whether to keep the owner's wording or add a floor.
  2. The insurer check comes first. R285-OWNER-INSURER-01 must confirm that a Cyber / Technology Professional Indemnity policy is in force, and its limit, before this cap is relied on in any customer contract.
  3. "Loss of data" in the Terms exclusion list. The Terms keep the existing exclusion of loss of data alongside the new data protection cap. Counsel to confirm the two read together as intended, so that a data protection claim is governed by the separate cap rather than excluded outright.
  4. Caps applied once. The DPA clause 11 and the Terms section 6 state the same caps. Counsel to confirm the wording makes them one set of caps, not two cumulative sets.
  5. UCTA reasonableness of the general cap (£2,500 floor) for small and mid size customers, public and private.
  6. Data subject claims. Liability that cannot be limited by law is left unlimited (clause 11.5). Counsel to confirm that covers direct data subject claims correctly.
  7. The remaining "for counsel" items in section 3: points 4, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 12.
